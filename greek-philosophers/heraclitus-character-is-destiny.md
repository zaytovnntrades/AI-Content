# Heraclitus — "Character Is Destiny" (1-min motivational Short)

**Length:** ~60s · **Aspect:** 9:16 · **Philosopher:** Heraclitus of Ephesus (c. 535–475 BC), "the Obscure"
**Key concept he foresaw:** everything is in constant change ("everything flows"), and your character, not luck, decides your destiny.

## Voiceover (~150 words, about 60s)
Twenty-five centuries ago, in the city of Ephesus, a man spoke in riddles so dark they called him Heraclitus... the Obscure.
He left his only book in the temple of Artemis, like a message to the future.
And the message was this: everything flows. Nothing stays the same.
You cannot step into the same river twice... because the river has changed, and so have you.
That sounds like loss. It's actually freedom.
If nothing is fixed, then neither is your past, your failures, or the person you were yesterday.
But change alone won't decide where you end up. Heraclitus gave the answer in three words.
Character is destiny.
Not luck. Not circumstance. The habits you choose, day after day, become the life you live.
The river is already moving. The only question is... who will you become while it carries you?

## Scenes (4 × 15s)
Shared look: painterly, like a classical oil painting brought to life; amber and deep teal palette; the same silver-bearded philosopher in a charcoal himation in every scene.
1. **0–15s, The Obscure:** at dawn he climbs the steps of a vast Ionic temple and lays a papyrus scroll on the altar. Slow push-in.
2. **15–30s, The River:** he steps barefoot into a fast river at golden hour as leaves swirl past. Low tracking shot.
3. **30–45s, The Fire:** on a night hilltop he tosses leaves into a brazier and the embers rise into the stars. Slow orbit.
4. **45–60s, Character Is Destiny:** he walks a stone path up a ridge at sunrise and stops at the summit. Crane up and pull back.
