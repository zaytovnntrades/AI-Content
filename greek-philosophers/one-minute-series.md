# One-Minute Philosophy Series: one thinker, one big idea, one payoff

Format, copied from the Heraclitus Short (`heraclitus-character-is-destiny.md`):
- **~150 words of voiceover, about 60 seconds**, in the same narrator voice every time (ElevenLabs "Ældier", eleven_v4)
- **4 scenes of 15 seconds each:** HOOK (who and where) → CONFLICT (the problem they faced) → IDEA (the quote or concept) → PAYOFF (what it means for *you*, ending on a question)
- **Shared look:** painterly, like a classical oil painting brought to life; amber and deep teal palette; same lighting style each episode
- **On-screen text:** only the quote and the final question. Turn on auto-captions in CapCut.
- **Every title follows one pattern:** *"The [Ancient] [Idea] That [Modern Benefit]"*

Episodes 1–3 are Greek. Episodes 4–5 are a **"Stoic Legends" spin-off**: Marcus Aurelius and Seneca were Roman, so the titles and descriptions say Roman.

## Ep 1: Epictetus, "The Only Thing You Control"
**Title:** The Slave Who Taught Emperors How to Be Free ⛓️ #stoicism
**Quote on screen:** "Some things are within our power, while others are not." (*Enchiridion* 1)

**Voiceover**
He was born a slave. His master once twisted his leg until it broke... and legend says he calmly replied, "I told you it would break." His name was Epictetus. He had no money, no freedom, no power over his own body. So he found the one thing no one could take. He wrote it in the first line of his handbook: some things are within our power, while others are not. Other people's opinions? Not yours. The economy? Not yours. The past? Not yours. But your effort, your choices, your reaction to whatever happens next... those belong to you. Completely. Epictetus went on to become one of the most famous teachers in the Roman world. A broken leg didn't stop him. So what's actually stopping you? Stop guarding what you can't control. Start building what you can.

**Scenes**
1. A Roman villa at dusk; a shackled young man in a rough tunic kneels on stone as a wealthy master looms in silhouette. Slow push-in.
2. A close-up of the broken shackles on a stone floor; the same man, now older, walks away limping into morning light. Low tracking shot.
3. An old teacher with a walking stick in a simple stone schoolroom, students around him, sunlight through a high window. Slow orbit.
4. A student standing alone on a cliff edge at sunrise, wind in his robe, steady and calm. Crane up and pull back.

*Fact note: the broken-leg story comes from later tradition (Celsus, quoted by Origen), so the script says "legend says." He taught in Nicopolis, and Emperor Marcus Aurelius later studied his teachings.*

## Ep 2: Diogenes, "Need Less, Be Free"
**Title:** The Man Who Owned Nothing and Feared No One 🏺 #diogenes
**Quote on screen:** "Stand a little out of my sun." (Plutarch, *Life of Alexander* 14)

**Voiceover**
He slept in a clay jar on the streets of Athens. He owned a cloak, a bag, and a single wooden cup. His name was Diogenes. One day he watched a boy drink water from his cupped hands... and he threw his cup away. "A child has beaten me in plain living," he said. People called him mad. But Diogenes had discovered something most of us never do: every new want is a new chain. When Alexander the Great, the most powerful man alive, stood over him and offered him anything, Diogenes looked up and said: stand a little out of my sun. Nothing to lose. Nothing to fear. You don't have to live in a jar. But ask yourself: how much of what you chase... is really chasing you? Want less. And watch how free you become.

**Scenes**
1. Dawn in an ancient Athenian street; a ragged bearded man crawls out of a huge tipped-over clay jar and stretches. Slow push-in.
2. A boy at a fountain drinking from cupped hands; the old man watches, then tosses a wooden cup aside, laughing. Side tracking shot.
3. A gleaming armored king and his soldiers cast a long shadow over the man sitting by his jar; he waves them aside to sit back in the sunlight. Low angle.
4. The old man lying back in warm sunlight, eyes closed, completely at peace, while the busy city rushes past. Slow pull-back.

*Fact note: the cup story is from Diogenes Laertius VI.37.*

## Ep 3: Socrates, "The Wisest Man Knew Nothing"
**Title:** The Oracle Called Him the Wisest Man Alive. Here's Why 🏛️ #socrates
**Quote on screen:** "The unexamined life is not worth living." (Plato, *Apology* 38a)

**Voiceover**
A friend once asked the Oracle at Delphi a dangerous question: is anyone wiser than Socrates? The Oracle answered: no one. Socrates was shocked. He didn't think he knew anything. So he went looking for someone wiser. He questioned politicians, poets, and craftsmen... the people everyone said were experts. And he found something strange. They all believed they knew things they didn't. Socrates realized his only advantage: he knew that he didn't know. That one insight made him the most dangerous man in Athens. He questioned everything, and he refused to stop, even when it cost him his life. The smartest people aren't the ones with all the answers. They're the ones who keep asking. So here's your question for today: what do you believe... that you've never actually tested?

**Scenes**
1. Mist-filled mountain temple at Delphi; a priestess sits above glowing vapor as a traveler kneels before her. Slow push-in.
2. Socrates, barefoot, bald and bearded, questioning a proud politician in a crowded marketplace; onlookers whisper. Handheld-style slow tracking.
3. Socrates sitting alone on temple steps at sunset, thinking deeply, a single oil lamp beside him. Slow orbit.
4. A young modern-looking student in a dim library opening an old book, warm light spilling over the pages. Crane up and pull back.

*Fact note: the Delphi story and his conclusion are from Plato, *Apology* 21a–d.*

## Ep 4 (Stoic Legends): Marcus Aurelius, "The Obstacle Is the Way"
**Title:** The Emperor's Secret Journal: What Blocks You Is the Path 🛡️ #stoic
**Quote on screen:** "What stands in the way becomes the way." (*Meditations* 5.20)

**Voiceover**
He was the most powerful man in the world: Marcus Aurelius, Emperor of Rome. War on the borders. Plague in the cities. Betrayal close to home. And every night, by the light of a campfire, he wrote private notes to himself... never meant for anyone else to read. Those notes became the book we call Meditations. One line in it changed how millions handle hard times: the impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way. A setback isn't a wall. It's training. Every problem you face is asking you to become someone who can solve it. Marcus didn't wait for an easier life. He used the hard one. So whatever is blocking you right now... what if it's the exact path you're supposed to take?

**Scenes**
1. A Roman military camp at night in a frozen forest; an armored emperor writes by candlelight in a tent. Slow push-in.
2. Rain and smoke over a battlefield and a plague-struck city; the emperor stands firm, cloak whipping in the wind. Slow tracking shot.
3. A giant boulder blocking a mountain path; a lone figure climbs over it, and the path beyond is revealed in golden light. Crane up.
4. Sunrise over the empire from a hilltop; the emperor closes his journal and looks forward. Slow pull-back.

## Ep 5 (Stoic Legends): Seneca, "Life Isn't Short. You're Wasting It"
**Title:** A 2,000-Year-Old Warning About Your Screen Time ⏳ #seneca
**Quote on screen:** "It is not that we have a short time to live, but that we waste a lot of it." (*On the Shortness of Life* 1)

**Voiceover**
Two thousand years ago, a Roman philosopher named Seneca noticed something that should scare us today. Everyone complained that life was too short. But Seneca disagreed. He wrote: it is not that we have a short time to live, but that we waste a lot of it. People guard their money, he said, but they give away their time to anyone who asks for it. Hours lost to things that don't matter. Days spent on someone else's priorities. Years waiting to start living. Seneca's answer was simple. Treat every hour like it's worth more than gold, because it is. You can earn more money. You can never earn more time. So look at how you spent yesterday... was it spent, or was it invested?

**Scenes**
1. A Roman writer at a marble desk by lamplight, quill moving across a scroll, an hourglass beside him. Slow push-in.
2. A busy Roman forum in time-lapse: people rushing, arguing and wasting the day as shadows sweep across the stone. Locked wide shot.
3. Close-up of an hourglass with golden sand pouring through, slowly morphing into gold coins slipping away. Macro, slow orbit.
4. The writer stands at a window at dawn, blows out the lamp, and begins the day with purpose. Slow pull-back.

## Production checklist for each episode (about 45 minutes once you've done it a few times)
1. Generate the voiceover (ElevenLabs flow, Ældier voice). ~5 min
2. Generate 4 stills or clips from the scene prompts. ~15 min
3. In CapCut: put the voiceover down first, cut the visuals to match it, add a slow zoom on each still. ~15 min
4. Add auto-captions, the quote card and a music bed ducked under the voice. ~5 min
5. Export at 1080×1920, upload with the title pattern above, and pin a comment asking the episode's question. ~5 min
