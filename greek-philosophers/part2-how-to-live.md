| 4 | #4 ZENO OF CITIUM (c. 334–262 BC) | Number four, Zeno of Citium. A merchant who lost everything in a shipwreck, then founded Stoicism on a painted porch in Athens. |
| 5 | "We have two ears and one mouth…" | His advice still hits: we have two ears and one mouth, so we should listen more than we speak. |
| 6 | #3 EPICTETUS (c. 50–135 AD) | Number three, Epictetus. Born into slavery, he became one of history's greatest Stoic teachers, proving the mind can stay free. |
| 7 | "It's not things that disturb us, but our judgments about things." | His core lesson: it is not things that disturb us, but our judgments about things. Change the story, change the feeling. |
| 8 | #2 DIOGENES (c. 412–323 BC) | Number two, Diogenes the Cynic. He lived in a giant clay jar in Athens and mocked wealth, fame, and fake manners. |
| 9 | "Stand a little out of my sun." | When Alexander the Great asked what he could do for him, Diogenes simply replied: stand a little out of my sun. |
| 10 | #1 EPICURUS (341–270 BC) | Number one, Epicurus. He founded a school called the Garden, teaching that true pleasure is simply freedom from pain and fear. |
| 11 | "Death is nothing to us." | To free his students from dread, he taught: death is nothing to us. So live without fear, and enjoy simple things. |
